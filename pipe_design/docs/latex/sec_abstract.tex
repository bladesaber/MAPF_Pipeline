\begin{abstract}
Additive manufacturing (AM) enables compact hydraulic components whose internal
fluid channels can follow free-form 3D paths rather than conventionally drilled
holes. High-performance rotary direct-drive servo valves are a representative
case: a motor-driven center axis must be connected to multiple groups of
external ports by short, smooth pipes that never collide with each other or
with structural obstacles. Once a channel is placed it permanently occupies
volume, so later channels must clear earlier geometry---unlike classical
multi-agent pathfinding (MAPF), where agents free space after moving.
We study this setting as \emph{multi-group pipe routing under permanent
geometric occupancy}. We formalize cross-group clearance conflicts (dual
witness points with radii), describe a layered planner that adapts CBS, PBS,
and priority planning to geometric constraints and multi-terminal topology,
and introduce a constructive 3D benchmark with two corridor generators and two
obstacle painters. On this suite we evaluate solvers by success rate under a
fixed time budget. The goal of this work is a reproducible problem definition,
benchmark, and baseline comparison for AM-oriented multi-group channel design.
\end{abstract}
