\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Discrete pipe routing.}
Belov et al.~\cite{belov2020pr} adapt MAPF-style search to 3D pipe routing;
surveys of plant-scale automatic routing emphasize sequential CAD and
MIP/sampling pipelines~\cite{blokland2023pipe}.
Our companion report~\cite{deng2025pipe_design} treats \emph{multi-group}
routing under permanent occupancy and supplies the coarse layouts we refine.
That work stops at discrete embeddings; continuous centerline cleanup is left
open.

\paragraph{Robotic path smoothing.}
CHOMP, STOMP, TEB, and TrajOpt~\cite{zucker2013chomp,kalakrishnan2011stomp,roesmann2013teb,schulman2014trajopt}
refine a single temporary trajectory under smoothness and collision costs.
We inherit the gradient-centric idea, but a one-path objective does not encode
shared trunks, permanent cross-group tubes, or the multi-usage junction conflict
of Sec.~\ref{sec:problem:junction}.

\paragraph{Fairing, CAD, and physics polish.}
Elastic-rod / curve fairing~\cite{moreton1992functional,bergou2008discrete}
and clothoid primitives~\cite{fraichard2004from} motivate soft bend energies on
one open curve; sparse circular-arc encodings are a poor fit for dense
grid-routed manifolds.
Parametric Hermite / NURBS centerlines are standard in CAD~\cite{piegl1997nurbs},
yet manual fillets do not resolve multi-usage joins under hard clearance.
Adjoint CFD / shape optimization sits \emph{downstream}: it typically deforms a
fixed channel layout and does not create a locally split junction
(Sec.~\ref{sec:problem:pipeline}).
Our contribution is the combination---branched, usage-coupled refine under
permanent tubular occupancy---not any single fairing or collision ingredient.
